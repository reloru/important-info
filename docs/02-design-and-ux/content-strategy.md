# Content Strategy

Most websites exist to communicate. The content — the words, images, and media —
*is* the product for the user. Yet content is routinely treated as an
afterthought, poured in at the last minute. Strategy fixes that.

## What content strategy covers

- **What** content you need (mapped to user needs and business goals).
- **Who** creates, reviews, and approves it.
- **How** it's structured, written, and formatted.
- **When** it's created, published, and reviewed.
- **How it's maintained** so it doesn't go stale.

## Content before (or with) design — not after

Designing around "lorem ipsum" and then squeezing real content in later almost
always breaks the design. Real content varies in length, has awkward edge cases,
and reveals structural needs. Draft real (or realistic) content early so design
and content shape each other.

## Writing for the web

People scan online. Write accordingly:

> ✅ **Do:**
> - **Front‑load** the important information (inverted pyramid).
> - Use clear, descriptive **headings and subheadings** that also form a logical
>   document outline (which helps SEO and accessibility). ♿
> - Keep paragraphs and sentences short.
> - Use **plain language** — write for your least‑expert reader.
> - Use lists and tables for scannable structure.
> - Write **meaningful link text** ("read the maintenance guide," not "click
>   here") — better for scanning, SEO, and screen readers. ♿
> - Maintain a consistent **voice and tone** aligned with the brand.

> ❌ **Don't:**
> - Bury the point under throat‑clearing introductions.
> - Use jargon or internal acronyms without explanation.
> - Write giant walls of text.

## Content, SEO, and accessibility overlap

Good content structure serves three goals at once:

- **Users** can scan and understand.
- **Search engines** understand your topic and hierarchy (headings, descriptive
  links, alt text). See [on‑page SEO](../06-seo/on-page-seo.md).
- **Assistive technology** users can navigate by headings and understand images
  via alt text. See [Accessibility](../04-accessibility/README.md).

Write once, well, and all three benefit.

## Images, media, and rights

> ⚖️ **Legal note:** Every image, font, video, and audio clip on your site
> needs a **license that permits your use**. Don't grab images from a search
> engine — that's copyright infringement, and "I found it online" is not a
> defense. Use properly licensed stock, Creative Commons content (respecting its
> terms), or original work, and **keep records** of licenses. See
> [copyright & IP](../10-legal-and-compliance/copyright-and-ip.md).

Also: provide **alt text** for meaningful images (accessibility + SEO), and
optimize media so it doesn't wreck performance (⚡ see
[image optimization](../05-performance/image-and-asset-optimization.md)).

## A content life cycle, too

Content isn't "done" when published. Plan for its life:

- **Governance** — who owns each area of content?
- **Review cadence** — schedule periodic reviews to catch stale info, dead
  links, and outdated claims.
- **Accuracy** — especially for anything legal, medical, financial, or
  price‑sensitive.
- **Archiving/removal** — retire content that no longer serves users (with
  redirects where needed — see [technical SEO](../06-seo/technical-seo.md)).

> 💡 **Tip:** Stale content quietly erodes trust and SEO. A recurring content
> review belongs in the [maintenance plan](../09-maintenance/maintenance-plan.md).

## Content inventory (for redesigns)

Before redesigning an existing site, inventory what's there: every URL, what
it's about, how it performs, and whether to keep, merge, rewrite, or cut it. This
prevents the classic redesign mistake of silently dropping valuable pages (and
their inbound links and rankings).
