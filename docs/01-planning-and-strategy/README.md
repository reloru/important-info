# 01 · Planning & Strategy

Every expensive website mistake traces back to a planning decision that was
never made — or was made implicitly, by accident. This section is about making
those decisions on purpose.

## Why planning matters

Changes get roughly **ten times more expensive** at each phase of the life
cycle. Renaming a section in a wireframe costs minutes; renaming it after launch
means redirects, SEO fallout, and broken links. Planning is not bureaucracy;
it's the cheapest place to be wrong.

## The planning checklist

- [ ] Goals and success metrics are written down and agreed.
- [ ] Target audience(s) are defined.
- [ ] Requirements are captured and prioritized (must / should / could).
- [ ] Information architecture (sitemap) is drafted.
- [ ] Content responsibilities are assigned (who writes what, by when).
- [ ] Technology approach and hosting are chosen with reasons recorded.
- [ ] **Legal obligations are identified early** — data collection, e‑commerce,
      target jurisdictions. (See [Legal & Compliance](../10-legal-and-compliance/README.md).)
- [ ] Budget includes launch **and** ongoing maintenance.
- [ ] Ownership of code, content, and accounts is agreed in writing.

> ⚖️ **Legal note:** The cheapest time to handle privacy and compliance is
> during planning. If you'll collect personal data or serve regulated
> jurisdictions, that shapes your architecture, your vendor choices, and your
> consent flows. Bolting it on later is painful and risky.
