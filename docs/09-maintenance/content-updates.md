# Content Updates & Link Rot

Content is the part of a website users actually come for — and it decays faster
than the code. Prices change, staff move on, links break, "latest" news becomes
years old, and yesterday's accurate statement becomes today's misleading one.
Keeping content current is a core, ongoing maintenance responsibility.

## Why stale content is a real problem

- **Trust** — outdated or wrong information makes a site (and business) look
  neglected or unreliable.
- **SEO** — search engines favor fresh, accurate content; dead links and errors
  hurt. See [SEO](../06-seo/README.md).
- **User frustration** — broken links, wrong hours, or outdated instructions
  waste people's time.
- **Legal/accuracy risk** — ⚖️ stale claims about prices, offers, product
  specs, or (worse) legal, medical, or financial information can create real
  liability. See [e‑commerce law](../10-legal-and-compliance/ecommerce-law.md).

## Link rot

Links break over time — internal pages get moved, external sites disappear or
reorganize. This is called **link rot**, and it's relentless.

> ✅ **Do:**
> - Run a **broken‑link check** periodically (there are automated tools) and fix
>   or remove dead links.
> - When you move or remove a page, add a **301 redirect** so inbound links and
>   SEO value aren't lost. See
>   [technical SEO](../06-seo/technical-seo.md).
> - Prefer linking to stable, authoritative sources for external references.
> - Fix your **internal** links first — they're fully in your control.

## Keep a content review cadence

Fold content maintenance into the [maintenance plan](maintenance-plan.md):

- **Monthly:** scan for broken links and obvious staleness; update
  time‑sensitive info.
- **Quarterly:** content audit — is each important page still accurate, useful,
  and current? Update, merge, rewrite, or retire.
- **Ongoing:** update immediately when facts change (prices, contact details,
  policies, product info).

## Retiring content responsibly

Old content sometimes should go — but don't just delete it:

- **Redirect** removed URLs to the most relevant replacement (301) so links and
  rankings transfer. See [technical SEO](../06-seo/technical-seo.md).
- For content of record you must keep but no longer promote, consider archiving
  or clearly dating it.
- Update internal links that pointed to the removed page.

## Governance: who owns content?

Content drifts when nobody owns it.

> ✅ **Do:** Assign ownership for each area of content, define a review schedule,
> and establish an editorial process (who writes, who reviews/approves, in what
> voice). See [content strategy](../02-design-and-ux/content-strategy.md) and
> [roles & responsibilities](../00-getting-started/roles-and-responsibilities.md).

## Accuracy for sensitive content

> ⚖️ Extra care for content where being wrong causes harm or liability:
> - **Prices, availability, offers** — must match reality (consumer‑protection
>   rules on misleading information). See
>   [e‑commerce law](../10-legal-and-compliance/ecommerce-law.md).
> - **Legal pages** (privacy, terms, cookies) — must stay current as laws and
>   your practices change. See [Legal & Compliance](../10-legal-and-compliance/README.md).
> - **Health, financial, safety** information — accuracy is critical; cite
>   sources and review regularly.

## Rights for new content

> ⚖️ Every time content is added, the same **licensing rules** apply: images,
> fonts, and media need permission for your use, and you should keep records.
> Don't let a rushed content update introduce an infringing image. See
> [copyright & IP](../10-legal-and-compliance/copyright-and-ip.md).

## The takeaway

Content is never "done." Schedule reviews, fix link rot, redirect what you
remove, assign clear ownership, and keep sensitive information scrupulously
accurate. A site that's demonstrably maintained earns trust; one full of dead
links and outdated claims quietly loses it.
