# Privacy Policy

A privacy policy is a public document that tells users **what personal data you
collect, why, how you use it, who you share it with, and what rights they have.**
If your site collects *any* personal data — and almost every site does, even via
a contact form or analytics — you very likely need one, both as good practice and
as a legal requirement in most jurisdictions.

> ⚖️ Not legal advice. This explains the concept and common contents; have a
> professional review your actual policy, especially if you handle sensitive
> data or operate across borders.

## Why you almost certainly need one

- **The law requires it.** GDPR, CCPA/CPRA, and many other laws mandate a privacy
  notice/policy. See [GDPR](gdpr.md) and [CCPA/CPRA](ccpa-cpra.md).
- **Third parties require it.** Analytics providers, ad networks, app stores, and
  payment processors typically require you to have one in their terms.
- **Trust.** Users increasingly expect transparency about their data.

"Personal data" is broad — names, emails, IP addresses, device identifiers,
location, cookies/analytics data, and more. If you collect it, disclose it.

## What a privacy policy typically covers

- **Who you are** — the controller's identity and contact details (and a DPO/EU
  representative if applicable — see [GDPR](gdpr.md)).
- **What data you collect** — categories (contact info, usage data, cookies,
  payment info, etc.).
- **How you collect it** — forms, cookies, analytics, third parties.
- **Why you use it** — the purposes (and, under GDPR, the *legal basis* for each).
- **Who you share it with** — service providers, analytics, ad partners,
  processors; and any international transfers.
- **How long you keep it** — retention periods or the criteria for them.
- **How you protect it** — a general description of your security measures. See
  [Security](../07-security/README.md).
- **Users' rights** — access, correction, deletion, objection, opt‑out, etc., and
  **how to exercise them**.
- **Cookies/tracking** — often here or in a separate cookie policy. See
  [cookies & tracking](cookies-and-tracking.md).
- **Children** — special rules apply if you might collect data from minors (e.g.,
  COPPA in the US; GDPR age‑of‑consent rules).
- **How to contact you** and how to complain (including to a supervisory
  authority under GDPR).
- **Effective date** and how you'll notify users of changes.

## Make it accurate and specific

> ✅ **Do:**
> - Describe **what you actually do** — a generic template that doesn't match
>   your real practices can be worse than none (it can be treated as a
>   misrepresentation). ⚖️
> - Write it in **plain, understandable language** (GDPR explicitly requires
>   clear, plain language).
> - **Link it prominently** — typically in the footer on every page, and at
>   points of data collection (forms, sign‑ups).
> - **Keep it current** — update it when your data practices change, and review
>   it periodically. See the annual review in the
>   [maintenance plan](../09-maintenance/maintenance-plan.md).

> ❌ **Don't:**
> - Copy a competitor's policy blindly — it describes *their* practices, not
>   yours, and may be legally wrong for you.
> - Promise things you don't do (e.g., "we never share data" while using ad
>   trackers).
> - Bury it or make it inaccessible.

## Privacy by design

The policy should *describe* good practices, not paper over bad ones. The
underlying principles (especially from [GDPR](gdpr.md)) shape how you build:

- **Data minimization** — collect only what you need. Every extra field is a
  liability. See [analytics](../06-seo/analytics.md).
- **Purpose limitation** — use data only for the purposes you disclosed.
- **Storage limitation** — don't keep data longer than necessary.
- **Security** — protect what you hold. See [Security](../07-security/README.md).

Building with these in mind makes writing an honest policy easy — and reduces
your risk.
