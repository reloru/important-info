# Copyright & Intellectual Property

Everything on a website — text, images, photos, fonts, videos, music, icons,
code, and design — is someone's intellectual property. Using it lawfully (and
protecting your own) is a core, frequently‑underestimated legal responsibility.
"I found it on Google" is not permission, and infringement can mean takedowns,
demand letters, and damages.

> ⚖️ Not legal advice. IP law is jurisdiction‑specific; get professional advice
> for anything significant.

## The default: copyright is automatic

Original creative works are protected by copyright **the moment they're
created** — no registration or © symbol required. So you should assume that any
image, text, font, or media you find is **copyrighted and off‑limits unless you
have permission** (a license) or a clear legal exception applies.

> ❌ **Don't:** Grab images from a search engine, copy text from another site,
> use a font you didn't license for web use, or drop in music/video without
> rights. This is infringement, even if the source didn't display a copyright
> notice, and even if you're not charging money.

## Getting the right to use content

Legitimate sources of content you can use:

- **Your own original work** — you hold the rights (subject to any client
  agreement — see [licensing](licensing.md)).
- **Licensed stock** — images, fonts, video, audio bought/subscribed under a
  license. **Read the license terms** — they limit how, where, and how much you
  can use it (web vs. print, number of copies, editorial vs. commercial).
- **Creative Commons / open licenses** — free to use *under conditions*. Respect
  them: many require **attribution** (BY), some forbid commercial use (NC), some
  forbid modifications (ND), some require share‑alike (SA). Using a CC‑BY image
  without crediting the author is a license violation.
- **Public domain** — works whose copyright has expired or been waived (e.g.,
  CC0). Free to use, though verify the status.
- **Explicit permission** — written permission from the rights holder.

> ✅ **Do:** **Keep records** of every license and permission — what you're
> allowed to do, and proof you have the right. If a claim ever arises, this is
> your defense. Track licenses for images, fonts, icons, video, audio, and any
> third‑party code.

## Fonts and web use

Fonts are commonly overlooked. A font licensed for **desktop/print** use is often
**not** licensed for **web embedding** — web use typically needs a separate
webfont license (or use of an open‑source font). Verify the license covers your
usage before embedding.

> 💡 Self‑hosting **open‑source** fonts (properly licensed) sidesteps both the
> licensing question and the [privacy issue](cookies-and-tracking.md) of remote
> font services, and it's often faster (⚡).

## Attribution and moral rights

- Some licenses (CC‑BY, many stock and open‑source licenses) **require
  attribution** — provide it as specified.
- In many countries, creators have **moral rights** (e.g., to be credited, and to
  object to derogatory treatment of their work) that persist even after a
  license.

## Trademarks are different from copyright

- **Copyright** protects creative *works* (an image, text, code).
- **Trademarks** protect brand identifiers (names, logos, slogans) that identify
  a source of goods/services.

> ⚖️ Check for **trademark conflicts** before choosing a business/domain name or
> using logos — using a mark that infringes an existing trademark can force a
> costly rebrand or worse. See
> [domains & hosting](../01-planning-and-strategy/domains-and-hosting.md). Don't
> use others' logos/marks in a way that implies endorsement or causes confusion.

## AI‑generated content: proceed carefully

> ⚖️ AI‑generated images, text, and code raise **unsettled** legal questions:
> whether the output is copyrightable, whether it infringes training data, and
> what the tool's terms permit. The law is evolving and varies by country. If
> you use AI‑generated assets commercially, understand the tool's license/terms
> and the current legal uncertainty, and keep records. Don't assume AI output is
> automatically free and clear.

## Protecting your own IP

Your site's original content, design, and code are *your* IP. Sensible steps:

- Include a **copyright notice** (e.g., "© 2026 Your Company") — not legally
  required, but it signals ownership and can affect remedies.
- Set clear **terms** on how others may use your content. See
  [terms of service](terms-of-service.md).
- For client work, make **ownership on delivery** explicit in the contract. See
  [licensing](licensing.md).
- Consider **registration** for valuable trademarks/works where it strengthens
  your rights (jurisdiction‑dependent).
- Use the **[DMCA](dmca.md)** notice‑and‑takedown process if others infringe your
  work online.

## Handling infringement (both directions)

- **If you receive a takedown/infringement notice:** take it seriously,
  investigate promptly, and remove or license the content as appropriate. See
  [DMCA & takedowns](dmca.md).
- **If someone infringes your content:** you can send a takedown notice or pursue
  other remedies.

## Practical checklist

- [ ] Every image, font, icon, video, and audio clip has a **license permitting
      your use** (and web use specifically for fonts).
- [ ] **Attribution** provided wherever a license requires it.
- [ ] **Records** of all licenses/permissions are kept.
- [ ] No copied text/design from other sites without permission.
- [ ] **Trademark** cleared for name/logo; no infringing use of others' marks.
- [ ] Your own content is marked and your ownership terms are stated.
- [ ] Client contracts spell out **who owns what** on delivery. See
      [licensing](licensing.md).
