# 10 · Legal & Compliance

Putting a website online creates real legal obligations. This is the section
people most often ignore — until a regulator, a lawsuit, or a takedown notice
forces the issue. This section explains the major areas of website law in plain
language so you know **what applies to you, what you must do, and when to get a
lawyer.**

> ⚖️ **This is not legal advice.** It's an educational overview to help you
> spot issues and ask the right questions. Laws vary enormously by jurisdiction
> and change frequently. For anything with real risk or money attached, consult
> a **qualified attorney** in the relevant jurisdiction. Do not rely on these
> summaries — or on templates — as a substitute for professional advice.

## The core idea: the internet is not a law‑free zone

A website is subject to the laws of the places its users are, not just where its
owner sits. If you have users in the EU, California, or elsewhere with strong
laws, those laws can reach you — regardless of where you're based. "I didn't know"
is not a defense.

## What applies to (almost) every site

Most sites, even small ones, need to consider:

1. **A privacy policy** — if you collect *any* personal data (contact forms,
   analytics, accounts). See [privacy policy](privacy-policy.md).
2. **Cookie/tracking consent** — if you use non‑essential cookies or trackers,
   especially with EU/UK users. See [cookies & tracking](cookies-and-tracking.md).
3. **Accessibility** — increasingly a legal requirement, not just good practice.
   See [accessibility law](accessibility-law.md).
4. **Proper content licensing** — every image, font, and clip needs the right to
   use it. See [copyright & IP](copyright-and-ip.md).
5. **Terms of service** — especially for accounts, e‑commerce, or user‑generated
   content. See [terms of service](terms-of-service.md).

## A practical way to approach compliance

1. **Map what you do:** What personal data do you collect, why, and where does it
   go? Do you sell? Serve which jurisdictions? Use which third parties?
2. **Identify which laws apply** based on your users and activities.
3. **Implement the requirements** (policies, consent, disclosures, security,
   accessibility) — ideally *during* [planning](../01-planning-and-strategy/README.md),
   not after launch.
4. **Document** your compliance measures — it demonstrates good faith.
5. **Review periodically** — laws and your site both change. See the annual
   review in the [maintenance plan](../09-maintenance/maintenance-plan.md).
6. **Get professional advice** where the stakes are real.

Compliance done early is cheap and routine. Compliance forced late — under a
complaint or breach — is expensive and stressful.
