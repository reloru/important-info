# Terms of Service

Terms of Service (ToS) — also called Terms & Conditions or Terms of Use — is the
**contract between your website and its users.** It sets the rules for using your
site, allocates risk, and protects you legally. Not every simple brochure site
strictly needs one, but any site with accounts, transactions, user content, or
meaningful functionality benefits from one.

> ⚖️ Not legal advice. A ToS is a legal contract; for anything beyond a basic
> site, have it drafted or reviewed by a professional.

## What a ToS typically covers

- **Acceptance** — how users agree, and that using the site means accepting the
  terms.
- **Eligibility** — who may use the site (age, jurisdiction).
- **Accounts** — registration, responsibility for credentials, termination.
- **Acceptable use** — what users may and may not do (no illegal use, no abuse,
  no scraping, etc.).
- **User‑generated content** — ownership, the license users grant you, and your
  moderation/removal rights (see below).
- **Intellectual property** — your ownership of the site's content and marks. See
  [copyright & IP](copyright-and-ip.md).
- **Purchases** — pricing, payment, refunds, cancellations (for e‑commerce, this
  overlaps heavily with [e‑commerce law](ecommerce-law.md)).
- **Disclaimers & limitation of liability** — limiting your legal exposure "as
  far as the law allows."
- **Warranties** — what you do and don't promise.
- **Indemnification** — users covering losses caused by their misuse.
- **Governing law & dispute resolution** — which jurisdiction's law applies and
  how disputes are handled (courts, arbitration).
- **Changes** — how you may update the terms and how users are notified.
- **Termination** — when and how access can be ended.

## Clickwrap vs. browsewrap (enforceability)

*How* users agree affects whether the terms are enforceable:

- **Clickwrap** — the user takes an **affirmative action** (ticks a box, clicks
  "I agree") before proceeding. **Much more enforceable.** ✅
- **Browsewrap** — terms are merely linked (e.g., in the footer), with no
  affirmative agreement. Courts are often **reluctant to enforce** these, because
  users may never have seen or agreed to them.

> ✅ **Do:** For terms that matter (accounts, purchases), use **clickwrap** —
> require an explicit "I agree," ideally with the key terms visible or clearly
> linked at that moment. Keep a record of acceptance.
> ❌ **Don't:** Rely on a footer link alone to bind users to important terms like
> arbitration clauses or liability limits.

## User‑generated content (UGC)

If users can post content (comments, reviews, uploads, profiles), your ToS should
address:

- The **license** users grant you to host and display their content.
- That users are **responsible** for what they post and must have the rights to
  it.
- Your right to **moderate, remove, or refuse** content.
- **Prohibited content** (illegal, infringing, harassing, etc.).
- A process for **copyright complaints** — see [DMCA & takedowns](dmca.md).

> ⚖️ Hosting UGC brings its own regime (intermediary liability / safe harbors,
> content‑moderation obligations under laws like the EU Digital Services Act).
> Understand your responsibilities if you host third‑party content.

## Disclaimers and liability limits (with limits)

ToS commonly disclaim warranties and cap liability. But:

> ⚠️ You **cannot contract out of everything.** Consumer‑protection laws in many
> jurisdictions void unfair terms and preserve certain consumer rights regardless
> of what your ToS says — you can't disclaim mandatory statutory rights (e.g.,
> some EU consumer guarantees). Overreaching clauses may simply be unenforceable.
> Draft to be reasonable *and* lawful, not maximally aggressive.

## Keep it readable and honest

> ✅ **Do:**
> - Write as clearly as the legal content allows.
> - Make the ToS **easy to find** (footer) and present it at key moments
>   (sign‑up, checkout).
> - **Date** it and keep a **version history**; notify users of material changes.
> - Keep it **consistent** with your [privacy policy](privacy-policy.md) and your
>   actual practices.

> ❌ **Don't:**
> - Copy a random template that doesn't match your business or jurisdiction.
> - Include clauses you don't understand or won't enforce.
> - Contradict your privacy policy or your real practices.
